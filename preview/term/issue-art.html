名詞解釋 ／ 藝術實踐的類型

議題本位藝術 Issue-based art

定義：以一個具體的社會議題為出發點與核心的藝術創作或計畫，用議題（而非風格或媒材）來界定作品。

說明

在這個平台裡，議題本位藝術指創作或計畫從具體的社會議題出發，例如環境、遷村、移工、社宅，而不只是用風格或媒材來分類。它包含居民一起參與的計畫，也包含以議題為題材的個人創作。

這是平台為了收錄案例而採用的工作定義。英文的 issues-based art education 在藝術教育領域已有成熟定義（見下段），但中文「議題本位藝術」當作泛稱時較鬆散；台灣學界常用的相近說法是「藝術介入」。有研究指出，這個中文詞的使用多建立在模糊的共識上，也和社會參與式藝術不完全相同。

在藝術教育的學理上，這個取向有更明確的根基。戴博拉·史密斯（Deborah Smith-Shank）在《國際藝術教育學刊》（2011）指出，『議題本位的藝術教育』是一種跨領域教學法：教師先帶出一個學生熟悉的重要社會議題（如生態、和平、核能，對年幼學生也可以是『早餐為何重要』或『玩具為何分男女』），再讓學生自己透過地理、歷史、科學等多元途徑調查——研究沒有預設程序，過程本身就是學會創作、談論與批判世界的方式。她主張以『物質文化』（material culture）為基礎：打破藝術與工藝、高雅與通俗的階級，讓學生從自身的地理、性別、社經位置出發，學會『解碼與編碼』身邊的物件。這條路特別重視認同，以及常被忽略的弱勢與女性聲音，核心提問是：『為何這重要？誰說它重要？弱勢與女性的聲音在哪？這滿足了誰的利益？』它內容豐富卻也有風險——教師不一定握有所有答案，而是與學生協同探究。

在國際藝術教育文獻裡，這個取向有明確的定義與操作方式。Atkinson（2004）把議題本位藝術教育界定為『一種教學法與實踐，學生透過藝術行動探索社會、文化與個人議題』，屬後現代藝術教育，把焦點從形式移向與當代生活相關的內容。Li（2018）以一堂談種族議題的中學藝術課示範了具體流程：先用當代／行動主義作品引發討論，提問時多用開放問題（你看到什麼？藝術家為何這樣畫、為何選這個顏色或字級？）而不急著給答案；接著讓學生以自己的方式創作行動海報、而非照抄藝術家；最後以『藝廊漫步＋便利貼互評＋分享創作過程』收尾，並提醒彼此尊重不同觀點。

Definition (English)

Definition: Art or projects defined by, and starting from, a specific social issue rather than by style or medium.

On this platform, issue-based art means works or projects that begin with a concrete social issue, such as environment, village relocation, migrant workers or social housing, rather than being classified by style or medium. It includes projects with residents' participation as well as individual works on an issue.

This is a working definition adopted by the platform to collect cases. In the art-education literature the English term 'issues-based art education' has an established definition (see below), though the Chinese label is looser as a general term. A closely related term often used in Taiwanese scholarship is "art intervention." One study notes that its use rests on vague consensus and is not quite the same as socially engaged art.

In art education this approach has a firmer scholarly basis. Deborah Smith-Shank, in the International Journal of Arts Education (2011), describes issue-based art education as an interdisciplinary method: the teacher first raises an important social issue familiar to students (ecology, peace, nuclear power - or, for younger pupils, why breakfast matters or why toys are gendered), then lets students investigate it themselves through many routes such as geography, history and science - there is no preset procedure, and the process itself is how they learn to make, discuss and critique the world. She argues for grounding it in material culture: breaking down the hierarchy between art and craft, high and low, and having students decode and encode the objects around them from their own geography, gender and class. It foregrounds identity and the often-ignored voices of women and the marginalized, asking: why is this important, who says so, where are those voices, and whose interests it serves. It is rich but risky - the teacher does not hold all the answers, but inquires alongside the students.

In the international art-education literature the approach is well defined. Atkinson (2004) calls issues-based art education 'a form of pedagogy and practice in which pupils explore social, cultural, and personal issues through art action', a strand of postmodern art education that shifts emphasis from form to content tied to contemporary life. Li (2018), in a middle-school class on racial issues, models a concrete sequence: open with contemporary or activist artwork; ask open questions (what do you see? why did the artist draw this, or choose this color or font size?) instead of giving answers early; let students make their own activist posters rather than copying the artists; and close with a gallery walk, sticky-note peer comments and sharing of the making process, reminding everyone to respect differing views.
