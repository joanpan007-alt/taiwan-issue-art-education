名詞解釋 ／ 藝術實踐的類型

民眾劇場／被壓迫者劇場 People's Theatre / Theatre of the Oppressed

定義：源自巴西波瓦（Augusto Boal）的參與式劇場取向，打破舞台與觀眾的界線、讓民眾成為行動者。

說明

《美育》第188期于善祿的文章說明，『被壓迫者劇場』（Theatre of the Oppressed）由巴西的奧古斯都‧波瓦（Augusto Boal，1931–2009）發展，主張拆除三道牆——舞台與觀眾席之間、戲劇場景與真實生活之間、藝術家與非藝術家之間——核心是讓每個人都能運用劇場的力量、成為自己生命的主宰。其中最為人知的『論壇劇場』（Forum Theatre）把社會與政治議題編成戲，讓觀眾上場改寫情節、一起演練解決之道。波瓦的《被壓迫者劇場》自 1970 年代起影響全球。

在台灣，這一脈常被稱為『民眾劇場』。同篇文章提到鍾喬是民眾劇場工作者，1990 年代中期關注外籍勞工、國際資本與社會改革，並與亞洲民眾劇場團體合作（鍾喬即差事劇團藝術總監）。平台案例中，石岡媽媽劇團（K51）就是差事劇團在 921 災後以民眾戲劇工作坊協力輔導而成。民眾劇場與『社區劇場』相近而有別：社區劇場重在以劇場凝聚特定社區，民眾劇場則更強調從被壓迫處境出發、以戲劇作為集體行動與培力。

Definition (English)

Definition: A participatory theatre approach descended from Brazil's Augusto Boal that breaks the line between stage and audience and turns people into actors.

Per Yu Shan-lu in Aesthetic Education no.188, the Theatre of the Oppressed was developed by Brazil's Augusto Boal (1931-2009), who called for tearing down three walls - between stage and auditorium, between dramatic scene and real life, and between artist and non-artist - so that everyone can use the power of theatre and become the master of their own life. Its best-known form, Forum Theatre, turns social and political issues into a play in which spectators come on stage to rewrite the plot and rehearse solutions together. Boal's Theatre of the Oppressed has influenced the world since the 1970s.

In Taiwan this lineage is usually called People's Theatre. The same article notes Chung Chiao as a People's Theatre practitioner who, from the mid-1990s, focused on migrant workers, global capital and social reform and worked with Asian People's Theatre groups (Chung is the artistic director of Assignment Theatre). Among the platform's cases, Shigang Mama Theatre (K51) was mentored into being by Assignment Theatre through People's Theatre workshops after the 921 earthquake. People's Theatre is close to but distinct from community theatre: community theatre centres on using theatre to bond a specific community, while People's Theatre stresses starting from conditions of oppression and using drama as collective action and empowerment. This entry follows Aesthetic Education no.188.
