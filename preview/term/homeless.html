名詞解釋 ／ 議題與社會背景

無家者／遊民 Homeless people / street people

泛指露宿街頭或沒有穩定住所的人；現行法規多用「遊民」，民間團體倡議正名為涵蓋更廣的「無家者」。台灣目前尚無專法明確定義。

說明

用詞上，中央社（2024-01-20）整理：『無家者、街友、寒士、城市流浪者都是遊民的代名詞』。政府與現行法規（如社會救助法、各地方《遊民安置輔導自治條例》）多沿用『遊民』；芒草心慈善協會等民間團體則倡議正名為『無家者』，以去除污名、並把定義擴大到不只露宿街頭的人。

依蕃薯藤 yam 新聞（2026-06-17）報導，芒草心專員王今暐說明研議中的《無家者專法》草案，擬把『無家者』擴大為五類：露宿街頭或公共場所者、居住於不宜人居住所者、安置於收容場所者、14 天內將被迫遷離而無替代方案者、因家暴等被迫逃離而無替代方案者，並涵蓋長期借住親友、頻繁更換暫住地的『居無定所者』。報導也指出致因不只是個人因素：受暴婦女的『隱性無家』、租屋市場歧視、安置資源城鄉落差、社會住宅等待過長等；台大社會系黃克先認為，台灣長期把無家者當成社福問題、而非住宅問題。

平台案例中，《貧窮人的台北》（K65）由向貧窮者學習行動聯盟自 2017 年起推動，讓無家者等貧窮經驗者以『創作者』身分訴說自己眼中的台北；給平凡人的萬華散策（K63）則有芒草心以無家者的紙箱創作參與。

要提醒的是：台灣目前尚無專法明確定義『無家者』，上述草案五類仍在立法倡議階段（國際人權兩公約審查曾要求政府於一年內制定專法）；衛福部或各縣市列冊遊民的正式人數統計，平台本次未取得可讀取的官方頁面，未列入。

Definition (English)

People sleeping rough or without stable housing. Current law mostly uses the word youmin (vagrants); civil-society groups campaign to rename them wujiazhe (homeless people), a wider term. Taiwan still has no dedicated law defining the group.

On terminology, CNA (2024-01-20) notes that homeless person (wujiazhe), street friend (jieyou), cold-and-poor (hanshi) and urban wanderer are all synonyms for youmin (vagrant). Government and current statutes (the Social Assistance Act and local vagrant shelter-and-guidance ordinances) mostly keep the word youmin; civil-society groups such as Homeless Taiwan (Mangcao Xin) campaign to rename them wujiazhe (homeless people), to remove stigma and widen the definition beyond those literally sleeping on the street.

Per Yam News (2026-06-17), Wang Jin-wei of Homeless Taiwan outlined a draft Homeless Rights Act under discussion that would widen homeless to five groups: people sleeping on the street or in public places; people living in places unfit for habitation; people housed in shelters; people who will be forced to leave within 14 days with no alternative; and people forced to flee domestic or other violence with no alternative — also covering the housing-insecure who long depend on friends or relatives or move constantly. The report stresses the causes are not merely individual: abused women’s hidden homelessness, rental-market discrimination, uneven shelter resources between cities and counties, and long social-housing waits. Huang Ke-xian (NTU sociology) argues Taiwan has long treated homelessness as a welfare problem rather than a housing one.

Among the platform’s cases, Poor People’s Taipei (K65), run by the Poverty Solidarity alliance since 2017, lets homeless people and others with experience of poverty speak of their own Taipei as creators; A Wanhua Walk for Ordinary People (K63) includes Homeless Taiwan’s cardboard-box creations.

A caveat: Taiwan still has no dedicated law defining homeless; the five-group draft above is at the advocacy stage (the two-covenants review once asked the government to pass a dedicated act within a year). Official counts of registered vagrants from the Ministry of Health and Welfare or the counties were not obtained from a readable official page this time, and are not included.
